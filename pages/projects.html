Projects

We've been at the forefront of the fight against crime, and we are proud to have played a role in helping to make Nigeria a safer place for everyone.


Project 01 - 1994

Vehicle Number Plate

Prior to 1994, Nigeria had no standard vehicle number plate. The plates were issued by the various car manufacturers and were of different sizes, colors, and designs. This made it difficult for law enforcement agencies to identify vehicles and track down criminals.

In 1994, our company innovatively introduced the standard vehicle number plates which was formally adopted by the Federal Government of Nigeria in the same year to ensure uniformity across the country.

This new design included a unique serial number for each vehicle, which made it easier for law enforcement agencies to identify vehicles and track down criminals.


Project 02 - 1999

Electronic Central Motor Register (ECMR)

In 1999, our company developed and implemented the Electronic Central Motor Register (ECMR) system for the Nigeria Police Force.

This system was created to help law enforcement agencies track down stolen vehicles and identify vehicles involved in criminal activities. The ECMR system has been instrumental in reducing vehicle-related crimes in Nigeria.

The ECMR system as we know today is the Nation's largest centralized database containing information about all registered vehicles in the country, including their owners, registration details, and vehicle specifications.


Project 03 - 2001

ICT Modernisation of the Nigeria Police Force (NPF)

As a step forward in the fight against crime, our company was contracted by the Nigeria Police Force in 2001 to implement a comprehensive ICT modernization project.

This project aimed to enhance the operational efficiency of the NPF by integrating advanced information and communication technologies into their daily operations.

Project 04 - 2016

Interpol i-24/7 Database Query Implementation

In 2016, we introduced the Interpol i-24/7 database query system which integrated with the Automated Travelers Information System (ATIS) and the Automated Lodger Information System (ALIS).

These systems provided real-time access to Interpol's global database, allowing law enforcement agencies in Nigeria to quickly and efficiently identify and track criminals across borders.

In 2026, these systems helped track down wanted terrorists responsible for Boko Haram attacks and financing of terrorism, thereby significantly enhancing national security in Nigeria.
